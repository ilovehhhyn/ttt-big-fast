## 1. Bottom line

**The mechanism the thesis needs has not been shown. What was read as evidence for it is mostly
the repair of a broken baseline. H1 is untested. As configured, the benchmark leaves almost no
room for the mechanism to show itself.**

The thesis inverts TTT-E2E on a pretrained Llama-3.2-1B with sliding-window attention: the fast
(test-time) weights are big, the full MLP matrices of the last 4 of 16 blocks (201,326,592
parameters), and the slow weights are small, a rank-64 LoRA plus norm gains and learned inner
learning rates. The LoRA is the slow weight set, meta-learned through the inner loop; it is not
a fine-tuning baseline with test-time training added on top (P17, "0.1 Hypothesis and arms"; R,
"What the `--inner-lr 0` run is, and is not").

Three measurements made on 2026-09-20 change how every earlier number reads.
- **The un-tuned windowed model is broken beyond the window.** Arm A scores 2.3279 inside the
  8192-token window and 4.13 to 4.21 beyond it; the SAME weights with full attention score about
  2.30 everywhere (R, "The full-attention diagnostic").
- **Context outside the window is worth about 0.02 nats.** Scoring the same tokens with a healthy
  model, with and without the older context: +0.0208 nats per book (95% CI [+0.0139, +0.0277])
  when 7168 or more recent tokens remain. That is the most ANY out-of-window memory can gain at
  T = 32768, k = 8192 on PG-19 with this model (R, "What context beyond the window is worth").
- **So arm B's gain is mostly repair, not memory.** With nothing trained, TTT improves loss by
  +0.1405 nats per book (95% CI [+0.1279, +0.1531], 22 of 22 books), all of it beyond the window
  (R, "TTT alone (arm B against arm A), nothing trained"). At most about 0.02 of that can be
  recovered information. Its position pattern, nothing in the window and more with distance,
  was read as the signature of a compressed memory; a fast-weight update that compensates for a
  broken model predicts the same pattern, because the breakage also exists only beyond the
  window (F §15). Where nothing is outside the window TTT is slightly harmful: -0.0016 nats per
  book (95% CI [-0.0020, -0.0011]) at T = 8192 (R, "The matched T/k = 1 row").

On a trained arm C, switching the inner loop off on identical weights costs +0.0248 nats per
book (95% CI [+0.0194, +0.0301], 22 of 22 books) (R, "Same weights, inner loop on and off"), and
arm C leads a plain LoRA fine-tune at the same 10-step budget by 0.044 nats, 2.6690 against
2.7125, one pair of runs with no seeds (R, "Arm C at 32K"). Both were measured on models that
are still broken beyond the window (about 2.83 there after 10 steps, against about 2.32
healthy), so neither can be credited to memory. They show that test-time training helps a
partly repaired model a little; they do not show what the thesis claims.

The comparison that isolates H1's own claim has now been made (section 4.12). Crossing how the
slow weights were trained with whether TTT runs at evaluation, the two effects are close to
additive: TTT is worth +0.0179 per book on meta-learned weights and +0.0164 on plain fine-tuned
ones, and training through the inner loop is worth +0.0247 with TTT on and +0.0232 with it OFF.
The interaction, whether training through the inner loop makes test-time training more useful,
is +0.0015 nats per book (95% CI [+0.0005, +0.0026]): real, and about a thirtieth of arm C's
lead. At this budget the slow weights are better for having been trained through the inner
loop, but not because they have learned to use the fast weights (R, "The 2x2 behind H1").

H1's falsifier (arm C ≈ arm B while arm D ≫ arm B) cannot be evaluated yet. Until 2026-09-20
arm D could not run at all (its slow set was empty). It does not fit one 80 GiB GPU at 32K with
arm C's settings; it fits at `truncate_bptt=1`, where a 10-step feasibility run reaches 2.7306
at outer lr 4e-5 and diverges at arm C's 4e-4 (section 4.14). Arm F is not implemented (R, "Arm D: the slow set was empty, and what
it costs to run"; RUN, guard in `build_everything`). Training so far is 8 to 10 outer steps at
32K, a budget R puts at roughly 300 to 600 times smaller than the reference's, with a
meta-gradient truncated to 2 of 32 inner steps (R, "Caveats"; R, "What TTT-E2E's protocol says
the baseline is").

The decision that matters most is where the thesis can be tested at all. A mechanism whose
ceiling is 0.02 nats cannot be separated from repair on a baseline that is half a nat from
healthy. Either the baseline is extension-trained until it is healthy beyond the window, which
is what the reference protocol does, and the comparison is made against that ceiling; or the
setting is changed so that more is at stake outside the window (a longer context, a smaller
window, or a task with real long-range dependence). Section 7 sets out the options.

## 2. The thesis and how it was to be tested

**H1, verbatim** (P17, "0.1 Hypothesis and arms"): "On a pretrained sliding-window Transformer, a
small slow set (attention LoRA + RMSNorm gains + per-tensor inner learning rates) meta-learned
through the inner loop captures most of the gain that TTT-E2E's all-weights outer loop provides
over TTT-naive, while the fast weights are the model's own MLP matrices (initialized from
pretraining, reset every sequence). **Falsifier**: arm C ≈ arm B while arm D ≫ arm B."

The preprint puts the test as: "The hypothesis is supported if (C) approaches (D) and both
exceed (B); it is falsified if (C) matches (B) while (D) does not." (TEX, "Method", "Arms and
metrics"). RQB's version of H1 includes MLP LoRA in the slow set; see section 8, B6.

## 3. What has been done

**Model and data.** Llama-3.2-1B from `unsloth/Llama-3.2-1B` ("ungated mirror, verified identical
config and token ids"); the chunked prefix+suffix path reproduces HuggingFace logits with max
abs difference 0.0000 and correlation 1.000000. DCLM documents of >=8193 Llama-3 tokens: 199.6M
train tokens, 902K val tokens = 110 sequences of 8192; PG-19 for 32K, token counts not recorded
(R, opening paragraphs). Arm E is a third-party 760M checkpoint converted from orbax (344
tensors, 883.7M params); its interleaved RoPE convention alone was worth 2.0 nats (R, "Arm E").
An evaluation contamination was found and fixed: PG-19's validation split opens with the King
James Bible (0.19 nats, 1,039 distinct tokens over 7K positions), so the split is now shuffled
with a fixed seed (R, "Corrected 32K numbers").

**Inner and outer loop.** Chunked inner loop with the loss taken before the update (Eq. 6),
functional fast weights, a second-order path, fast weights reset every sequence (P18, "Core
mechanism"). Gates passed: inner lr = 0 reproduces the no-TTT gradient exactly; checkpoint group
size is numerically inert; gradients flow through the KV cache (F §11). At 1B scale the full
machinery with inner LR 0 gives 2.4939 against arm A's 2.4940 (R, "The three things this table
says").

**Memory work that made 32K fit.** At 8K the binding cost is the attention double backward
through the math SDPA backend: backward grows about 12 GiB per additional fast block, forward
under 2 GiB, and `fast_blocks=4` peaks at 77.30 GiB (R, "What makes fast_blocks=4 fit at 8K"). At
32K the frozen prefix is segmented with a rolling KV cache (exact; in one shot it holds 72 GiB)
(R, "The regime matters"). Two more fixes followed: truncated BPTT had been a no-op for memory
until each window was differentiated as soon as it closes (gradients unchanged, pinned by
`test_per_window_backward_matches_single_backward`), and each prefix segment is now checkpointed
individually. F §13 also records that an earlier probe was itself wrong and retracts two
conclusions of F §12.

| context | truncate_bptt | resident across sequence | peak | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16K | 0 (exact) | 7.11 -> 39.20 GiB | 77.45 | OOM in backward |
| 16K | 2 | flat 8.73 GiB | 55.39 | completes |
| 32K | 2 | flat 8.86 GiB | 49.57 | completes |
| 32K | 4 | - | 77.45 | OOM |

(F, "13. Why arm C really OOMed, and the two fixes".) The price: "arm C at 32K optimises a biased
objective". Arms A and B and all evaluation are unaffected (F §13 "Cost").

**Robustness and reproducibility** (F, "14. Cross-check against the reference implementation,
and robustness fixes").
- A warmup that rounds to zero is a hard error at config construction. Evaluation no longer
  builds a meta-gradient: 32K evaluation went from out-of-memory to 11.5 GiB peak.
- A non-leaf tensor shared by every truncation window was freed by the first window's backward
  (the prefix output, then `exp(inner_lr_log)`); both are now cut at a detached leaf, and step
  time went 455.8 -> 131.9 s/step. The tests are parametrised over optimizer x learned_lr and
  "were checked to fail with the cluster's exact error when each fix is removed".
- Exact checkpoint/resume, on by default. On real hardware (job 14174410) a run killed with
  SIGKILL resumed at step 4/10; against the uninterrupted `C_32k_q10` (job 14163396) the
  evaluation is 2.66892 against 2.66895. The largest gradient-norm difference, 1.7% at step 8,
  is within run-to-run noise: a second uninterrupted run differs from the reference by 1.2% at
  the same step (section 4.13; R, "Checkpoint / resume on real hardware").
- `--arm F` refuses to run rather than "report arm C's numbers under arm F's name" (RUN).

**Evaluation and statistics tooling.** Per-sequence losses in result files; `--eval-ttt-off`; a
document-clustered paired test (`ttt/eval/paired.py`, `scripts/paired_ttt_effect.py`, with
`--baseline` to pair two result files); a per-token-index breakdown; `scripts/compare_runs.py`; a
forgetting probe reachable from the CLI, "exercised only on SmolLM2-135M" (R, "Runs in flight").

**Tests.** F §11 records 140 tests on 2026-09-18. `pytest --collect-only` on this machine today
lists 180 cases (my sum of its per-file counts, `hf`-marked tests deselected). I did not run the
suite.

**Added on 2026-09-20 after the first version of this report** (git log; R, "2026-09-20, later";
F §15).
- Checkpoint/resume is settled on real hardware: the killed-and-resumed run lies inside the
  noise of two uninterrupted runs (section 4.13). `--ckpt-every N` exists for arms with large
  checkpoints; a run killed BETWEEN checkpoints was verified to resume bit-exactly on CPU.
- Two arms could not have run. Arm F was a copy of arm C's configuration and now refuses to
  run. Arm D's slow set was EMPTY (`slow_spec=("**",)` was matched as a substring); it is now a
  wildcard for every non-fast parameter, tested on the split and with a real training step.
  In this arm the fast weights' initialisation is not trained and no weight decay is applied.
- `--inner-lr` no longer has a default when an inner optimizer is active. The old default, 1e-3,
  is the value that drove the loss to 20.2.
- `--load-slow` evaluates trained slow weights under a chosen inner rule (weights only, `--mode
  eval` only, provenance recorded). It is what makes the 2x2 of section 4.12 measurable.
- `scripts/context_value.py` measures what out-of-window context is worth to a healthy model
  (section 4.11). `scripts/render_tables.py` prints result tables from the result files with
  each row's settings read from its own arguments. `scripts/check_report_numbers.py` checks
  that every decimal number in a report occurs in the project's records; this report passes
  it, and a copy with one altered result does not.
- Every GPU job script requests 80 GiB cards; the nine pending jobs were fixed in place.

### 4.5 Arm C at 32K and the `--inner-lr 0` control (MEASURED)

PG-19, T = 32768, k = 8192, b = 1024, `fast_blocks=4`, normalized SGD at `lr_rms = 4e-6`, LoRA
r = 64, outer lr 4e-4, 131,072 tokens per step (4 sequences), `truncate_bptt=2`, same 32
validation sequences (R, "Arm C at 32K"):

| run | training | inner loop at eval | loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| arm A | none | off | 3.7119 |
| arm B | none | on | 3.5694 |
| plain LoRA fine-tune (`--inner-lr 0`) | 10 steps | off | 2.7125 |
| arm C | 10 steps, through the inner loop | on | 2.6690 |

What the control does not mean: R rejects the reading "0.999 nats from LoRA, 0.044 from TTT".
The LoRA "IS the slow weight set". With the inner loop disabled the meta-gradient "collapses to
ordinary next-token loss" and the run learns a different, plainly fine-tuned LoRA. "The two runs
do not share slow weights, so nothing can be subtracted."

What it does mean: a system-level comparison, "at an equal budget of 10 steps, the full method
beats plain LoRA fine-tuning by 0.044 nats", and most of the distance from arm A is not specific
to this method (R, "What the `--inner-lr 0` run is, and is not"). No interval is given for the
0.044.

### 4.8 The inner learning-rate unit (MEASURED, confirmed against the reference config)

The reference inner rule is `clip_by_global_norm(1.0)` then `sgd(lr=1)`. In this codebase's
per-element RMS unit, a unit-norm step over n_fast elements is 1/sqrt(n_fast) = 7.05e-5 for
n_fast = 201,326,592. The plan derived its scale from the paper's 11.5M-parameter prime MLP and
was 14x too large. The unit "is now confirmed from their config rather than inferred"; the 32K
optimum is 4e-6, 0.057x theirs (F §14 "The inner-LR unit"). A per-element step "is NOT
transferable across fast-weight set sizes" (P18, "Correction"). The reference update has
global norm min(||g||, 1), at most 1, so 7.05e-5 is the per-element size of their LARGEST
possible step and 0.057x is relative to that maximum (F §14, as corrected).

### 4.10 Which baseline is fair (the reference repository, as reported by R)

### 4.13 Run-to-run noise, and the cheaper control (MEASURED)

Two UNINTERRUPTED runs of the same 10-step arm C configuration on different nodes differ by up
to 2.49e-4 in per-step loss (mean 7.8e-5), by 1.2% in gradient norm at step 8, and by 1.4e-4 in
evaluation loss (2.66909 against 2.66895). The run that was killed with SIGKILL and resumed
differs from the same reference by LESS (max 1.73e-4; evaluation 2.66892), and its first
difference of any kind occurs at step 0, before any checkpoint existed. Resume is therefore
within run-to-run noise, and "two runs of this configuration agree to about 2.5e-4 nats" (R,
"Checkpoint / resume on real hardware"). This is the noise of re-running one configuration on
one seed; variance across seeds is still unmeasured.

`--inner none` reproduces `--inner-lr 0` on real hardware at 32K (largest per-step loss
difference 2.5e-4, evaluation 2.71244 against 2.71249) and is 1.9x faster (68.7 against 131.3
seconds per step), so extension-only controls can skip the double backward (R, "What
TTT-E2E's protocol says the baseline is").

## 5. What this means for the thesis

**What the evidence supports.**
- The machinery works. A 201,326,592-parameter fast set made of the model's own MLPs can be
  updated at test time, differentiated through, trained at 32K on one GPU, checkpointed and
  resumed within run-to-run noise (sections 3, 4.13). The correctness gates are strong (HF
  parity; lr-0 reproduction to four decimals).
- Test-time training reliably lowers the loss of a model that is not functioning beyond its
  window, with nothing trained (+0.1405 per book) and, by a smaller margin, on a partly
  fine-tuned model with identical weights (+0.0248 per book) (4.3, 4.6). Both intervals exclude
  zero and both effects are positive in 22 of 22 books.
- It does nothing, or very slightly harms, when nothing is outside the window (-0.0016 per book
  at T = 8192; +0.0013 worse on top of full attention) (4.2, 4.9). That is the behaviour a
  context mechanism should have.
- Arm C leads the budget-matched plain fine-tune by 0.044 nats at 10 steps (4.5).

**What cuts against it, or limits it.**
- None of the gains above can be credited to the mechanism the thesis is about. Context
  outside the window is worth about 0.02 nats to a healthy model (4.11); the un-tuned windowed
  model is about 1.85 nats from healthy beyond the window, and the 10-step fine-tuned models are
  still about half a nat from it (4.9). Every positive TTT number so far was measured on a
  broken or partly repaired model, where compensating for the damage and remembering context
  predict the same position pattern (F §15).
- The ceiling is small in absolute terms. Even a perfect out-of-window memory gains about 0.02
  nats at T = 32768, k = 8192 on PG-19 with this model. Showing the thesis in this setting
  means resolving effects of that size against a healthy baseline.
- The 0.044-nat lead is one pair of 10-step runs with no seeds. Run-to-run noise for one
  configuration on one seed is about 2.5e-4 nats (4.13); variance across seeds is unmeasured.
- Training is short: 8 to 10 outer steps at 32K, where the reference extension-trains every
  model for 725 steps at 32 sequences per step (4.10).
- The meta-gradient spans 2 of 32 inner steps and is biased. How much that matters is PENDING
  (`C32k_t1`) (R, "Caveats").
- Un-tuned arms A and B are outside the reference protocol (4.10).
- AdamW, a required arm, reaches about two thirds of normalized SGD's best as arm B and has
  not been used in a meta-trained arm (4.7).
- Numbers are on the project's own split with PG-19 in place of Books3: "not comparable to
  their published tables" (R, "Caveats"). Arm E is 8K only, third-party, not parameter-matched.
- Forgetting is unmeasured on the real model, although the design overwrites the pretrained MLP
  that the paper keeps static as "safe storage" (F §3 item 3; R, "Runs in flight").

**Relation to H1's falsifier.** Neither the falsifier ("arm C ≈ arm B while arm D ≫ arm B") nor
the support condition can be evaluated. Arm D had an empty slow set until 2026-09-20 and had
never been runnable; with the settings arm C uses it runs out of memory at 32K in step 1, and
the only arm D numbers are from a 10-step feasibility run at a different truncation and
learning rate (4.14). **The experiments needed to evaluate H1's falsifier have not been run.** The C-versus-B half exists
only in weak forms: C better than B by 0.0641 at 8K after 18 steps; C indistinguishable from B
at reduced scale; and at 32K a raw gap (2.6690 against 3.5694) that the control shows is mostly
reachable by fine-tuning with no inner loop.

**What H1 is really about, and the comparison that isolates it.** H1 claims that slow weights
meta-learned THROUGH the inner loop capture the gain. The 2x2 of section 4.12 tests this at a
fixed 10-step budget, and its answer is unfavourable so far: the interaction is +0.0015 nats
per book, against a training effect of +0.0232 that is present with TTT switched off. Training
through the inner loop gives better slow weights, for a reason the docs do not explain, but
there is almost no sign that those weights have learned to steer the fast-weight updates. One
run per row, 10 steps, models still in the repair regime: this can change with training, and it
is the measurement to repeat as the budget grows.

**Also unknown.**
- Whether "big" matters. Nothing compares big with small fast weights under one regime: arm F
  is not implemented and the fraction ablation stops at 1/4.
- Whether arm C's lead grows, holds or vanishes with more steps or larger batches (PENDING),
  and, for each of those runs, how much of any gain is repair.
- How the method compares with TTT-E2E at 32K: no measurement exists.
